# GeoCities / Web 1.0

The "personal homepage circa 1998" aesthetic. Designed to look like an enthusiast made it themselves on a Saturday afternoon.

## Why it works (when it works)

This aesthetic reads as a real person, not a brand. In an FB/IG feed full of polished ads, a GeoCities-style graphic stops the scroll because it's *off-brand by design* — it looks like someone's hobby project, which signals authenticity, niche-community-membership, and zero marketing budget (which paradoxically reads as trustworthy for small/local/handmade/indie offers).

Best used for: indie products, local events, niche communities, crafts/makers, small services where the owner *is* the brand.

## Color palette

Pick one combo per ad:

- Neon green (`#00FF00`) + Cyan (`#00FFFF`) + Magenta (`#FF00FF`) on **black**
- Rainbow gradient on black or starfield background
- Hot pink (`#FF69B4`) + Lime (`#7FFF00`) on patterned (tiled) background
- Sepia/parchment background with red and blue text (collegiate / "personal site" energy)

Backgrounds are rarely white. Black, starfield, or a tiled pattern (bricks, clouds, stars, weave) is more authentic.

## Typography

- **Headline**: Comic Sans MS, or a similar friendly script. Bold. Often with a multi-color rainbow gradient applied per letter. 70-100pt.
- **Subhead**: Times New Roman or Courier. Italic for "fancy."
- **Body**: Comic Sans or Arial. Mixed case. Often centered.
- **"Marquee" banner**: Pixel-style or bold sans, scrolling-text energy (but static, since this is a PNG).

The Comic Sans is non-negotiable for the headline. It's the signature.

## Distinctive elements

- **Tiled background**: A small repeating pattern (stars, bricks, clouds, weave). Render as a tiled bitmap behind everything.
- **Rainbow text**: The headline has a different color per letter or per word. Cycle through bright primaries.
- **Faux-marquee banner**: A bordered box at top with pixel-style decorations (★, ✦, ~, *) flanking the text.
- **"Under construction" energy**: An icon, a caution-stripe band, or a "🚧" emoji somewhere. Not necessarily literal.
- **Faux hyperlink CTA**: Blue, underlined, with brackets like `[Click here!]`. The "link" includes the actual contact info.
- **Visitor counter**: A small bordered box at the bottom with a 5-digit number, styled like a 90s page counter.
- **ASCII / Unicode decorations**: `~ * ~`, `<>< ><>`, `:::`, `★彡`, used as dividers.

## Copy voice

Enthusiastic, personal, slightly over-sharing. Written like someone who's *into* what they're selling and assumes you are too.

**Examples:**
- "★ welcome to my soap shop ★ handmade in [city] since 2019 ★"
- "~*~ vintage pyrex! ~*~ updated weekly! ~*~ DM to buy! ~*~"
- "🚧 BAND PRACTICE SPACE FOR RENT 🚧 — east side, $20/hr, drummers welcome"

The bullets should read like a personal manifesto:
- "• Every bar made by hand in my kitchen!"
- "• I use real essential oils, no fragrance oils!!!"
- "• Local pickup or USPS Priority — your choice :)"

The CTA is a faux-hyperlink with the actual contact: `[Email me!]` or `[DM @handle]` or `[Click here: phone number]`.

## What to avoid

- Don't make it *so* authentically ugly that the offer disappears. The visual is the hook; the offer still has to land.
- Don't use this for serious/professional services where the lo-fi vibe undermines trust (lawyers, accountants, surgery).
- Don't fake "personal" — this aesthetic works because it telegraphs a real human. Don't use it for a brand that's clearly a brand.
